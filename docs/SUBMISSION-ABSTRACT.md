EVIDENCELOCK studies a practical safety problem: when evidence is missing, contradictory, corrected or withdrawn, can a small multilingual model change its decision appropriately?

We build a revision-aware evidence ledger with explicit timestamps, source authority, correction relationships and scope. An independent oracle assigns four states: SUPPORTED, MISSING_EVIDENCE, CONFLICT and OUT_OF_SCOPE. Responses are evaluated for decision correctness, supported facts, citation validity, unresolved issues and permitted next actions.

The reproducible pipeline chains CuratorKIT for traceable curation, AlignTune for genuine LoRA training of CohereLabs/tiny-aya-fire, and AuditKIT for evaluation of preserved model predictions. Our original synthetic benchmark contains 297 English cases with separate 99-case training, validation and frozen-test partitions grouped by family/template lineage. A paired supplement adds 99 Hindi and 99 Hinglish questions derived from the same frozen scenarios while retaining their English evidence and oracle targets.

On the frozen English test, the auxiliary state classifier improves from 39/99 to 77/99 correct decisions. Conflict detection improves from 0/9 to 9/9. Incorrect SUPPORTED decisions on the 27 missing-evidence cases decrease from 24 to 6, a 75% reduction in this measured error count. Four-rotation unanimity rises from 10/99 to 94/99. These improvements are classification results: six unsafe missing-evidence decisions remain, and separate complete-response and paired-language evaluations reveal substantial drafting and schema-reliability gaps.

The release preserves 693 complete-response generations, 792 auxiliary option-score records, raw prompts, dataset lineage, native toolkit reports, all 12 available unique adapter artifacts and immutable reproduction inputs. These observations come from 99 underlying frozen scenarios and specified model comparisons, not independent new scenarios. No frozen-test outcomes were used to tune model weights, prompts, labels or decoding.

EVIDENCELOCK contributes an executable safety-research pipeline, an original revision-aware benchmark and auditable before-and-after evidence that targeted decision learning can improve while complete-response reliability remains a separate challenge. The study supports a narrow synthetic pilot; the small translation review was model-assisted rather than independent native-speaker validation.
